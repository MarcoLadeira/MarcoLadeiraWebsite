The product

Overview

SaveThePlanet was built by a four-person team at Hack the Climate in Dublin. We used Irish grid data to explore whether flexible charging could use energy that might otherwise be turned down.

The problem

The grid can sometimes generate more renewable power than it can use or move. A flexible vehicle can wait for a better charging window if it still has time to reach the driver’s required charge.

Why it mattered to me

We wanted to turn a grid forecast into a decision someone could actually understand and act on.

My role

  • Set the product scope, prioritised the demo and coordinated a four-person multidisciplinary team.
  • Helped connect the forecasting, charging plan and dashboard into one prototype.
  • Worked through an incentive model that explains how benefits might be shared if the approach were deployed.

Architecture

Hard problems

A good forecast is not a feasible charge

The optimiser checks every plan against hard constraints — site power limits, charger counts and rates, and each car's arrival, departure and energy need — and reports why a vehicle could not be served, most fundamental reason first. A tempting forecast that the fleet can't actually absorb is shown as such.

No look-ahead, no wishful thinking

The demo replays real history, so it would have been easy to cheat. Offers lock 30 minutes before a window and are chosen from predictions only; +60 forecasts are never mixed with +30. Tests enforce both rules.

Decisions & trade-offs

Use caseEV charging as the first flexible loadBatteries, heat pumps, data centresFocused and demoable in days; other loads left as next steps.
IncentiveShare the incremental saving 50 / 25 / 25 (driver / operator / platform)Flat discountDrivers get a reason to shift; offers are rejected when ordinary smart charging is already cheaper.
Demo reliabilityKnown-good replay scenario as fallbackLive feeds onlyThe story works even if a network dependency fails on stage.

Testing & reliability

We replayed historical scenarios and checked the scheduling constraints with automated tests. The browser also includes offline example data for demonstrations.

Lessons

  1. Start with the constraints that matter to the driver and the charging equipment, then optimise within them.
  2. Keep historical data, forecasts and simulated outcomes labelled separately so the demo is easy to interpret.

Project status

The prototype is available as source code. It was demonstrated with historical data and simulated scenarios. It has no measured real-world charging or emissions outcome.
