The product

Overview

A run gives you a board of Oddlings and a rule to satisfy. Swap two pieces, check whether the board now fits, then move through more boards as the run continues.

The problem

A short puzzle has to feel fair immediately. The player needs to understand the goal and recognise what changed after a swap.

Why it mattered to me

I wanted a game that could make sense in a few seconds and still draw me back for the next daily puzzle.

My role

  • Worked on the rules, characters’ presentation, daily runs, touch controls and the browser interface.
  • Built deterministic board generation and replay links so a run can be reproduced.
  • Built a separate board checker and on-device difficulty adjustment.

Architecture

Hard problems

Every board must be fair

The generator builds a finished picture, then breaks it with one swap. That is not enough on its own: a bug could still ship an unsolvable board. A separate checker re-reads each board as literal rows and columns — no gameplay helpers — and confirms at least one swap satisfies the visible goal. Any swap that satisfies the goal is accepted, not just the generator's. A quality script builds 500 runs of 40 boards (20,000) and re-checks every one; the last run had zero failures.

Testing & reliability

An independent checker validates the generated boards. I also test keyboard, pointer and touch controls through browser runs.

Lessons

  1. The most useful feedback is close to the piece you moved, especially on a small screen.
  2. Independent verification helps distinguish a difficult board from one the generator should never have made.
