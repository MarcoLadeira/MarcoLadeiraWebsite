Lab · Experiment 01

Capacity Lab

Back-of-the-envelope system sizing, with the maths showing. Give it traffic and per-node assumptions; it sizes each tier, shows where load lands, and lets you break things to see what fails first.

What this is not: it doesn't design systems from a sentence, and it isn't a benchmark. It's the first hour of a design review, made interactive. Every formula is listed at the bottom and every default can be changed.

The maths

  1. average rps = daily users × requests per user ÷ 86,400
  2. peak rps = average rps × peak-to-average, split into reads and writes by the read share
  3. app instances = ⌈peak rps ÷ (instance rps × target utilisation)⌉ + 1 — the +1 is a spare so losing one is survivable
  4. database reads = peak reads × (1 − cache hit rate); replicas are sized at the normal hit rate, so a cold cache shows how far over they'd go
  5. shards = ⌈peak writes ÷ (primary writes/s × target utilisation)⌉
  6. queued writes = peak writes × failover pause; they drain at the primaries' spare capacity
  7. storage per year = daily writes × write size × 365 × copies; egress uses average, not peak, traffic

Real systems are messier: hot keys, uneven shards, connection limits, garbage-collection pauses and cross-region latency all bite before the averages say they should. The point of the lab is to make the first-order shape of a design visible — and to show why a cold cache is a database problem.
